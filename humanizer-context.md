# Brand context for the humanizer skill

The skill auto-loads this file from the project root and treats it as an
extension of the chosen `--voice`. Its patterns are the source of several of
the AI-tell rules in `config.REFUSAL_RULES`.

Voice lives here. The facts live in `BRAND.md`, with citations. If the two
ever disagree, `BRAND.md` was read from the live site and this file was not.

## What we sell

JotPsych is the behavioral health EHR that defends clinicians from insurance
companies. Notes, billing and the practice in one system: it drafts the chart
during the session, checks the note and the claim against payer rules, works
the denials, and follows the money to the bank account.

It started in 2023 as an ambient scribe and is not one any more. Anything
written here that treats the note as the whole product is out of date, and for
a win-back list it is worse than out of date, because the scribe is the thing
these people already left.

The named parts, and there are no others: **AI Scribe**, **JotBill** (claims,
denials, appeals), **JotCred** (payer enrollment), **JotAudit** (flags a weak
note before it is signed), **JotSite** (practice website), **JotRx**
(e-prescribing, EPCS), **JotMeet** (telehealth).

## The antagonist

Worth knowing because it sets the register. The brand is not aspirational, it
is adversarial on the clinician's behalf, and the opponent is named: payers.
Denials, clawbacks, the gap between the care given and the dollar collected.
The verbs carry it. Defends. Scans. Works the denials. Follows every dollar.

Nothing in JotPsych's own copy promises a better life. It promises a better
position against a specific opponent. Copy that drifts back toward
work-life-balance language has left the brand, even when every individual word
is clean.

## Who we're writing to

Behavioral health clinicians: psychiatrists, PMHNPs, psychologists,
therapists, counselors. Mostly solo or small private practices. They run
their own businesses, carry licensure risk, and are drowning in notes.

They are not startup people. They do not read tech marketing. The thing
that gets their attention is a specific, accurate description of their
Tuesday evening.

## Voice

`professional`, leaning dry. Peer-to-peer, not vendor-to-lead.

## Banned phrases

Beyond the skill's 43 patterns, never write:

- "revolutionize" / "transform your practice" / "game-changer"
- "unlock" anything
- "seamless" / "effortless" / "frictionless"
- "leverage" as a verb
- "in today's fast-paced healthcare landscape"
- "AI-powered" as a bare adjective (say what it does instead)
- any em dash (absolute rule from the skill)

## Preferred terms

| Don't write | Write |
|---|---|
| providers | clinicians (or the specific credential) |
| users | clinicians |
| patients | depends on segment: prescribers say patients, therapists often say clients |
| solutions / platform | the chart, the note, the claim, JotPsych |
| utilize | use |
| documentation burden | notes, paperwork, charting |
| insurance headaches | denials, clawbacks, the specific payer |
| revenue cycle | getting paid for the work |
| our AI | the scribe, or what it actually does |

The site says "clients" throughout. Our rule stays narrower on purpose: the
site is writing to a room, we are writing to one person whose credential we
looked up, and getting it right for that person is worth more than matching
the homepage.

## Things a real clinician would notice

- A therapist doing 50-minute sessions and a psychiatrist doing 15-minute
  med checks have nothing in common workflow-wise. Writing to both at once
  reads as someone who has never met either.
- "Saves you time" is what every vendor says. "Your notes are done before
  your next session starts" is a claim about their actual day.
- Anything that sounds like the software is making clinical decisions is
  disqualifying, not just off-brand.
