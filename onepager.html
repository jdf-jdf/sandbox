Go-to-market case · JotPsych

Second Timing

A win-back machine for the ~15,000 clinicians who tried JotPsych and stopped. Its most important output is the mail it refuses to send.

Jo Flores · one page, recommendation first

Note

Quick version of what it does: it reads a list of people who tried JotPsych and stopped, figures out who each one actually is (prescriber or therapist, solo or institutional), decides which of them we can say something true and specific to, writes to those and only those, and refuses to send anything that would embarrass us.

Refusing to send is most of the value. A campaign optimizes for volume out the door. This optimizes for not burning goodwill on a mail-merge these clinicians would spot in two seconds.

A few things worth your five minutes:

  • The 24-rule gate is the part I'd stress-test first. It blocks fabricated stats, invented conversations ("as we discussed"), PHI mentions, hype words, the whole list. Run python tools/gate_demo.py and you'll see it catch six of seven planted bad drafts in real time, with the exact line that tripped it.
  • There's one call in here I want your read on. Institutional clinicians (hospital, health system) get written to instead of suppressed. They can't buy today, but they leave for private practice constantly, and the one who can't buy this year picks their own tools the year after — so silence costs us that person permanently, and writing costs us one email. That's SUPPRESS_INSTITUTIONAL = False in config.py. Flip it and rerun if you want to see the suppressed numbers versus what the machine resolves on its own, which is the version I actually care about long-term.

What it can't do yet: it doesn't know about the warm funnel sitting in the CRM or in your heads (new SDR territory, a partner intro, someone who opened four emails and never replied). That's a column on the intake CSV whenever you're ready to feed it in. Happy to walk through any of it live, or just tell me where to point it next.

Recommendation

Don't build a re-engagement campaign. Build a machine that earns the right to write, and that can prove a return.

  • Timing is the problem, so volume isn't the answer. Writing to all 15,000 burns the list before the timing turns. The machine refuses anyone it can't say something true and specific to, and records why.
  • "Notice the moment it does" is plumbing, not reporting. Solved at send time or not at all. Every send carries a token on all three doors back: the link, the reply address, the phone number.
  • Three columns aren't a segment. Name, email, mobile say nothing about who prescribes or who can buy. So the machine researches the employer once per domain, then the person where that isn't enough, caches both, then decides.
  • Generation is the thinnest layer. The model can't choose who to write to, can't pick a segment, can't place the tracking link. Anything a rule can get right isn't left to a model.

The zero

A gate that never fires is indistinguishable from no gate

The prompt forbids these failures upstream, so the model rarely commits them. Right for the product, bad for evidence. So fixtures test the gate directly:

BLOCK D-01  fabricated_stat         '90% reduction'
BLOCK D-02  fabricated_relationship 'You asked for'
BLOCK D-03  phi_leak                'a patient who'
BLOCK D-04  compliance_overclaim    'completely private'
BLOCK D-05  clinical_claim          'recommends a diagnosis'
BLOCK D-06  hype + fake_urgency + em_dash
pass  D-07  clean draft, correctly untouched

Six blocked on nine rule hits, one clean draft through. Feeding those blocks back is what puts never write '90% reduction' at the top of the next run.

Human time

Where the one to two hours a month go

Not on reviewing what it wrote. Only work genuinely waiting on a person reaches the queue — one row out of 33 — so clearing it is ten minutes. The rest of the hour goes to what the machine can't do for itself:

  • Tell it where the warm funnel is. It can research a domain and rank a list. It can't know we just put an SDR in Portland, that a partner introduced us to a twelve-clinician group, or who opened four emails and never replied. That lives in the CRM and in people's heads — and it goes in as a column on the intake CSV.
  • Argue about positioning with GTM, out loud. The brand brief, approved claims and segment briefs are human-authored files. That's where a conversation about story and register becomes something executed identically across 4,000 clinicians. It beats proofreading because it changes every future send, not one.
  • Run it, and read what came back. The loop only improves if someone reads the output and writes the rule. fabricated_relationship exists because a person read a draft that had invented a conversation.
Week two

What comes next, in this order

  1. Wire the collectors. Tokens and resolution are real; the web log, inbox and SMS webhook are simulated. A day of integration makes every number above live.
  2. Fix the run counter. Derived at the end rather than claimed at the start, so an aborted run reuses it and re-mints existing tokens. That's how 27 sends became 29.
  3. Cadence and a real second touch, so "stay alive until the timing turns" is a sequence over months, not a repeat.
  4. Use the mobile column for a low-frequency, high-signal touch to someone who ignored two emails.
  5. Feed returns back into targeting. The report knows which segment returns best; nothing uses it yet.
Scope

Real

  • Intake, both research passes, their caches
  • Routing, suppression, the 23-rule gate, quarantine
  • Multipart email over live SMTP
  • Attribution tokens, ledger, returns resolution
  • Review queue, metrics, learning loop, cron

Sketched, and marked

  • Inbound collectors (sample signal file)
  • The person cache — sample clinicians are invented, so every seeded record says "source": "simulated"
  • SMS as a sending channel

What breaks first, unattended: the person cache going stale. Titles expire, so verdicts age out into the review queue rather than silently staying true. Correct, but the queue grows quietly and the first symptom is a rising needs_review count.

Swap data/lapsed_clinicians.csv, rerun, and every number here changes. That is the test of whether it is a machine or a picture of one.
